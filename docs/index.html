The run, in numbers

Computed from the per-frame robot transform (680 frames, 28.3 s at 24 fps). Raw data: robot_path.csv, metrics.json.

The robot accelerates from 0.12 to 0.69 m/s over frames 120–449, reaching y = −4.95 m. At frame 449 it reverses with no deceleration (speed drops from 0.69 to 0.29 m/s in one frame; this is an animation keyframe step, not a physical turn) and returns at a steady 0.29 m/s. Heading changes only 12.9° over the whole run, so it reverses along one axis rather than turning round (inferred from the yaw track).

The LiDAR sensor model

The scan is a procedural shader, not ray-cast geometry. These values are read from the LiDAR Wave node group in LiDAR_Pass (lidar_params.json).

SLAM

No SLAM was run for this demo, so there is no SLAM trajectory, map or error figure here. What exists is the ground-truth robot path above (exact, from the animation) and five AprilTags in the scene (ids 14, 15, 16, 18, 19) that a SLAM or tag-based localisation test could use. The next step would be to feed the Scan_Camera frames to a tracker and compare its trajectory against robot_path.csv.

How the demo was made

Two halves: getting Isaac Sim's warehouse into Blender without losing its look, then adding a LiDAR that looks like a LiDAR. Code for the first half is in tools/ and addon/. The second half lives in the .blend, which is not in this repo.

Flatten the USDThe warehouse is World0.usd from Isaac Sim 5.0 (Digital Twin Warehouse). usd_flatten_collect.py flattens the stage into one warehouse.usdc and copies every texture it can find (88 collected, 0 copy failures).
Read the MDL materialsOmniverse materials are MDL. mdl_parse.py pulls texture paths and parameters out of the MDL files so they can be rebuilt, rather than guessed from a viewport colour.
Map to Blender shadersmaterial_spec.py classifies each material into a template (73 omni_pbr, 12 constant, 5 ue4_basic, 5 ue4_mask_lerp, 3 ue4_desat_tint, 2 ue4_color_mask4, 1 ue4_emissive) and base_materials.py builds the matching Principled BSDF graph.
Import and patchblender_import.py (or the isaac_usd_importer add-on) imports geometry and applies the graphs. overrides.json patches what USD cannot supply, such as the chain-link fence opacity (made by make_chainlink_alpha.py).
Verifyverify_export.py and render_check.py compare the export with the source, with lit and unlit previews. Result: 101 materials converted, 63 textured, 38 constants, 15 left as authored.
Add robot and sensorA robot-vacuum model animated along the aisle (robotAction, frames 0–800). A LiDAR_Sensor empty is parented to it, with its own action from frame 120. A fisheye camera (Scan_Camera, equisolid, 21 mm) sits at the sensor.
Paint the scanA second view layer, LiDAR, forces the LiDAR_Pass material on everything. Its shader turns distance and angle from the sensor into a ring-and-dot pattern: 480 × 40 returns, 14 m range, 14° up and 38° down, with a 140° trailing glow behind the sweep.
RenderCycles with the OptiX denoiser on an RTX 4070 Ti. Each clip frame is rendered once per camera and layer, then encoded to MP4 and GIF with ffmpeg. Clips use every 8th frame (86 frames at 12 fps), so they play faster than real time.

Honest labels. A Blender render of the Isaac warehouse with an animated LiDAR visualisation. Not an Isaac Sim sensor run and no point cloud. 54 source textures lived on an Omniverse Nucleus server and were not localised, so those materials fall back to constants. Clip frames were rendered at 12 samples for speed.

NVIDIA warehouse assets and textures are not included in this repo and keep their own licence.
